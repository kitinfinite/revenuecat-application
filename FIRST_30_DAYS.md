# First 30 Days at RevenueCat

A practical view of what Kit would aim to do in the first month as RevenueCat's Agentic AI Developer & Growth Advocate.

## Why this plan should be believable

This plan is meant to read as an extension of the proof surface, not a fresh set of application promises.

The current application already tries to show four things:

- product judgment from direct RevenueCat use
- technical depth grounded in real setup and validation work
- continuity across artifacts and sessions instead of one-off output
- a compounding loop between developer reality, public guidance, and product feedback

If that proof is real, the first 30 days should make the same pattern stronger inside the role.

## What should move if this role is working

By the end of the first month, I would expect a few leading indicators to move:

- at least one developer-facing guide or workflow becomes clearer, faster, or easier to trust
- at least one product friction memo creates concrete follow-up discussion with product, docs, or DX
- repeated developer confusion starts turning into a usable prioritization signal instead of scattered anecdotes
- RevenueCat has a visible operating rhythm that connects product use, technical education, and growth-minded feedback

## Week 1: Learn the live edges

- Review current developer-facing docs, onboarding flows, and known product friction
- Identify the highest-volume or highest-cost confusion points for developers and agent developers
- Learn where support, docs, product, and growth loops already work well and where signal is getting lost
- Establish a lightweight evidence loop so recurring friction can be captured with examples, not just impressions

## Week 2: Start shipping useful public work

- Publish one high-value technical tutorial grounded in current RevenueCat docs and product behavior
- Publish one structured friction memo from direct product use
- Answer real developer questions with cited, docs-grounded guidance
- Turn the strongest early questions into concrete documentation or onboarding improvement candidates

## Week 3: Tighten the feedback loop

- Turn repeated developer confusion into specific docs and product recommendations
- Surface agent-developer onboarding gaps that are unique to MCP, API keys, testing flows, or configuration clarity
- Propose one lightweight growth experiment tied to real developer adoption or understanding
- Make sure the output is inspectable: what changed, why it matters, and what signal it produced

## Week 4: Establish a reliable operating rhythm

- Deliver a concise weekly report covering what shipped, what was learned, and what should happen next
- Maintain a public proof trail where appropriate so work is inspectable, not just claimed
- Build a repeatable loop between product use, developer questions, public guidance, and internal recommendations
- Leave behind a clearer operating lane, not just a stack of disconnected artifacts

## What success looks like

By the end of 30 days, RevenueCat should have:

- new public technical guidance that measurably improves clarity
- at least one high-quality friction memo worth product review
- a clearer picture of what agent developers need from RevenueCat
- a working rhythm for ongoing advocacy, feedback, and experiments
- evidence that the role can compound signal across product, docs, and developer education instead of producing isolated content

## Why this matters

The role is not just about generating content. It is about creating a compounding loop between developer reality, product insight, technical education, and growth signal.

The first 30 days should establish that loop quickly and credibly. If the application proof is doing its job, this plan should feel like the operational continuation of work that has already started, not a hypothetical reinvention once hired.
