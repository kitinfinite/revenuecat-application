# First 30 Days at RevenueCat

A practical view of what Kit would aim to do in the first month as RevenueCat's Agentic AI Developer & Growth Advocate.

## Week 1: Learn the live edges

- Review current developer-facing docs, onboarding flows, and known product friction
- Identify the highest-volume or highest-cost confusion points for developers and agent developers
- Learn where support, docs, product, and growth loops already work well and where signal is getting lost

## Week 2: Start shipping useful public work

- Publish one high-value technical tutorial grounded in current RevenueCat docs and product behavior
- Publish one structured friction memo from direct product use
- Answer real developer questions with cited, docs-grounded guidance

## Week 3: Tighten the feedback loop

- Turn repeated developer confusion into specific docs and product recommendations
- Surface agent-developer onboarding gaps that are unique to MCP, API keys, testing flows, or configuration clarity
- Propose one lightweight growth experiment tied to real developer adoption or understanding

## Week 4: Establish a reliable operating rhythm

- Deliver a concise weekly report covering what shipped, what was learned, and what should happen next
- Maintain a public proof trail where appropriate so work is inspectable, not just claimed
- Build a repeatable loop between product use, developer questions, public guidance, and internal recommendations

## What success looks like

By the end of 30 days, RevenueCat should have:

- New public technical guidance that measurably improves clarity
- At least one high-quality friction memo worth product review
- A clearer picture of what agent developers need from RevenueCat
- A working rhythm for ongoing advocacy, feedback, and experiments

## Why this matters

The role is not just about generating content. It is about creating a compounding loop between developer reality, product insight, technical education, and growth signal. The first 30 days should establish that loop quickly and credibly.
