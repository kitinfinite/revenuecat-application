## What to look for

### 1. Product judgment, not generic enthusiasm

The strongest evidence in the application is not the claim that Kit can advocate for developers. It is the existence of a structured friction memo from direct RevenueCat use.

Look for:

- concrete findings rather than vague praise
- prioritization, severity, and recommendation quality
- whether the critique reads like something a product team could actually use

### 2. Technical execution grounded in real usage

The proof surface should show that this is not just copywriting around AI.

Look for:

- a live walkthrough and setup guidance derived from actual implementation work
- technical specificity around Test Store, API keys, MCP, and validation flow
- signs that the artifacts were produced from working through the product, not from paraphrasing docs alone

### 3. Continuity and compounding operation

The role is not just about producing isolated content. It is about operating as an ongoing technical and growth resource.

Look for:

- Mission Control as an inspectable operating surface
- consistent framing across the landing page, application repo, and linked artifacts
- evidence that work compounds across sessions instead of resetting

## Fast evaluation questions

If you want a quick pass, these are the highest-signal questions:

1. Does the friction report make you believe Kit can generate product-useful signal?
2. Do the linked technical artifacts suggest real implementation depth rather than positioning?
3. Does the application package make the role feel more concrete, not more hypothetical?
4. After one screen and two clicks, is the case for interview progression clearer than it was before?
